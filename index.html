Emergency guide

Mission medical procedures come first. These steps are a short reminder, not a diagnosis. If someone is unresponsive and not breathing, start CPR and call the crew medical officer now.
Heart attack or chest pain

Signs: Pressure, tightness or pain in the chest. Pain spreading to the arm, jaw or back. Shortness of breath, cold sweat, nausea or feeling faint.

  1. Stop all activity and secure yourself in a stable, comfortable position.
  2. Alert a crewmate immediately. Do not stay alone.
  3. Loosen tight clothing and breathe slowly.
  4. Connect the heart monitor and pulse oximeter from the medical kit, if available.
  5. Contact the flight surgeon and follow the mission medical checklist, including any medication it names.
  6. If the person becomes unresponsive and is not breathing, start CPR and use the defibrillator.
Cardiac arrest (unresponsive, not breathing)

Signs: Does not respond, is not breathing normally, no pulse.

  1. Call for help and alert the whole crew now.
  2. Start chest compressions and follow the mission CPR procedure.
  3. Attach the defibrillator and follow its voice prompts.
  4. Contact the flight surgeon. Continue until help takes over or the person recovers.
Irregular or racing heartbeat

Signs: Heart pounding, skipping beats or beating very fast while resting. May come with dizziness or chest discomfort.

  1. Stop activity and secure yourself. Breathe slowly.
  2. Count your pulse for 30 seconds and write it down.
  3. Check blood oxygen if you can.
  4. Notify the crew. Contact the flight surgeon if it lasts more than a few minutes, or if you feel faint, dizzy or have chest pain.
  5. Do not exercise until the flight surgeon clears you.
Trouble breathing or low oxygen

Signs: Short of breath at rest, blue lips, confusion, or a blood oxygen reading below 90%.

  1. Stop activity and breathe slowly.
  2. Check cabin air alarms and ventilation, and follow the procedure for a clean air supply.
  3. Check blood oxygen if you can.
  4. Notify the crew and contact the flight surgeon now.
  5. Use supplemental oxygen from the medical kit if your checklist says so.
Fainting, confusion or severe headache

Signs: Passing out, sudden confusion, or a very strong headache. Low oxygen and high carbon dioxide can cause these.

  1. Secure the person so they cannot drift into equipment.
  2. Alert the crew and check breathing and pulse.
  3. Check blood oxygen and cabin air alarms.
  4. Contact the flight surgeon and follow the checklist.
  5. Avoid critical tasks until cleared.
Fever or chills

Signs: Body temperature above 38 °C (100.4 °F), shivering or feeling very hot.

  1. Measure your temperature.
  2. Rest and drink fluids. Limit close contact with crewmates.
  3. Notify the crew and contact the flight surgeon.
  4. Record the symptoms in the next daily log.

Contact the flight surgeon for any serious symptom.
